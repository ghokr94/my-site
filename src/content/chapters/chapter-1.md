A few years ago, I was living with two friends, both of whom were die-hard sports fans. One has a Liverpool FC tattoo on his arm. The other hit .600 in high school and was getting Division 1 looks in baseball and loves the Yankees.

But when I'd put on a YouTube deep-dive on the living room TV about Liverpool's tactics against Barcelona in the 2019 Champions League, or how groundskeepers engineer stadium grass to survive through a brutal winter, neither of them would watch with me.

"Why not?" I'd ask. They loved these sports. They lived them.

And that's when it clicked. There are levels to this.

They were fans. Real fans. And I was something else entirely. The behind-the-scenes, the business, the tiny details nobody else notices or cares about; that's when I knew I couldn't look away from a career in sports. I'm the guy who nerds out over the fact that the mud coating every MLB baseball comes from New Jersey soil. And as a proud New Jerseyan, that means more to me than it has any right to.

That obsession was trying to tell me something. It took me a while to listen to it.

Back in 2019, I was working on Wall Street, selling software for a SaaS startup. On paper, the dream was real: New York City, sales, Wall Street. But in reality, my heart wasn't in it at all.

In my guide, I called myself a cactus in a rainforest. That SaaS job may as well have been in the deepest parts of the Amazon. And I was a saguaro, a desert plant, slowly wilting away.

I stopped ignoring the obsession and did something about it.

Fast forward a couple of years. I was no longer on Wall Street in tech sales. I'd graduated from the Football Business Academy with my master's…and I was a salesman again. This time, however, for the Colorado Rapids, in a sport I ~~loved~~ was obsessed with.

The work wasn't easy. Selling over the phone is still selling over the phone. But passion changed everything about how I showed up. My manager at the Rapids once pulled me aside and asked me to promise I'd work my f\*\*\*ing ass off. So, I did. I climbed the leaderboard, converted at a level I didn't know I had in me, and earned a promotion within three months of picking up the phone in Colorado (how that actually went down is a different story for a different day).

Do you need to love what you do to be successful? No.

But in my experience, the work feels a million times easier when you love what you talk about day-in-day-out.

That's the whole point. Passion on its own isn't a plan. But you can get started in two easy steps:

**First**, get honest about what you're actually obsessed with, the thing you'd study for free while everyone else is tuning it out.

**Second**, decide to chase it.

Find your passion. Look at the sports roles you could see yourself in ten or fifteen years from now, and work backwards. Your dream career, and the long road into it, starts with the first step.
